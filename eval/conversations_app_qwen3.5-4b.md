You are looking at **Page 46** of your book, which covers the concept of **Average Rate of Change** (تغییر اوسط). Since you asked to "solve it" without specifying which exercise, I will solve **Exercise 1, Question 1** from the notes on this page for you.

The problem asks us to find the ratio $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$ and the slope of the curve for the function $f(x) = 2x^2 - 4$ at $x = 0$.

First, let's find the expression for $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$. We calculate the change in $y$ ($\Delta y$) by subtracting the function value at $x$ from the function value at $x + \Delta x$:
$$ \Delta y = f(x + \Delta x) - f(x) $$
$$ \Delta y = [2(x + \Delta x)^2 - 4] - [2x^2 - 4] $$
Expanding the squared term gives us $2(x^2 + 2x\Delta x + (\Delta x)^2) - 4 - 2x^2 + 4$.
Simplifying this, the $-4$ and $+4$ cancel out, and $2x^2$ cancels with $-2x^2$, leaving:
$$ \Delta y = 4x\Delta x + 2(\Delta x)^2 $$
Now, we divide by $\Delta x$ to get the ratio:
$$ \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = 4x + 2\Delta x $$

Next, we find the **slope of the curve** (the limit of this ratio) by letting $\Delta x$ approach 0:
$$ \text{Slope} = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} (4x + 2\Delta x) = 4x $$
Finally, we substitute $x = 0$ into our slope equation:
$$ \text{Slope at } x = 0 \text{ is } 4(0) = 0 $$

So, the ratio is $4x + 2\Delta x$ and the slope at $x=0$ is $0$.
